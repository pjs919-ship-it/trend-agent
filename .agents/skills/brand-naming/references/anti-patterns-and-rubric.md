# Naming Anti-Patterns, Korean Localization & Evaluation Rubric

## 1. Anti-Patterns That Kill Names Immediately

### A. AI & Konglish Slop (Hard Reject)
| Pattern | Examples | Why It Fails |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Category + Modifier Compounds** | `트렌드핏`, `브랜드앵글`, `숏폼레이더`, `DataSync`, `CloudGuard` | Feels like a dated B2B brochure; describes the shelf but creates zero recall. |
| **Hollow First-Position Fillers** | `Smart~`, `AI~`, `Auto~`, `Meta~`, `Hyper~`, `Pro~`, `Next~`, `Win(ning)~` | Consumes prime real estate while signaling "generic AI wrapper." |
| **Hollow Second-Position Fillers** | `~랩(Lab)`, `~허브(Hub)`, `~플로우(Flow)`, `~싱크(Sync)`, `~스택(Stack)`, `~핏(Fit)`, `~데스크` | Interchangeable across 1,000 tools; fails the swap test. |
| **Suffix Addiction / Fake Latin** | `-ly`, `-ify`, `-ora`, `-ix`, `-ex`, `Luminex`, `Synaptiq` | Obviously AI-generated; hard to remember after one glance. |
| **Over-Explaining When Subtitle Exists** | Repeating `뷰티`, `마케팅`, `AI` when the tagline already states it | Bloats the header and cheapens brand voice. |

### B. The Five Fatal Flaws
1. **Boring Descriptions** ("유튜브 쇼츠 마케팅 분석기") — clear for 5 seconds, forgotten forever. Use the tagline for clarity, the name for recall.
2. **Real-Word Neglect** — real words average ~68.8% recall vs ~38.1% for arbitrary coinages; invented words must come from clear morphemes (e.g., `Figma` ← figure, `Vercel` ← versatile+accelerate).
3. **Swap Test Failure** — if the name works equally well on a logistics ERP or a fitness app, it has no specific metaphor.
4. **Semantic Self-Contradiction** — strong primary metaphor undermined by slang, awkward acronym, or negative secondary meaning.
5. **Misspellings for Domain Hacks** — dropping vowels or swapping `C/K` blindly fails the Phone Test unless phonetically clean (`KUE` works because spoken = "큐").

### C. Red Flags Checklist (2+ "Yes" = Disqualify)
- [ ] Does it sound like "another [10 existing tools]"?
- [ ] Missing a 15-second visual origin story?
- [ ] Requires spelling out letter-by-letter after hearing it once?
- [ ] Awkward to say aloud in `"방금 [name]에서 뽑았어요"`?
- [ ] Both halves of a compound fail the synonym replacement test?
- [ ] Only justification is "domain was free"?

## 2. Korean & Bilingual Localization Rules
- **No Forced Konglish Hybrids** unless culturally effortless: avoid 5–6 syllable English transliterations (`브랜드마켓핏`, `캠페인스튜디오`) for sleek daily SaaS tools.
- **Length Sweet Spot**: Korean native nouns **1–2 syllables** (`결`, `안목`, `결판`) or clean English **1–2 syllables / 3–6 letters** (`KUE`, `VERVE`, `FOLIO`, `CLAY`).
- **Sound Symbolism**: Crisp plosives/affricates (`ㅋ/ㅌ/ㅍ`, `K/P/T/C`) feel sharp, editorial, tactical; soft sonorants (`ㅁ/ㄴ/ㅇ/ㄹ`) feel skin-warm, refined, cosmetic. Match the sound to brand archetype.
- **Korean Check Mandatory**: Verify unintended Konglish jokes, Naver brand-search crowding, and KIPRIS class 9/35/42 similarity before recommending.

## 3. Contextual Sentence Tests (Every Finalist Must Pass)
- **Word of Mouth**: `"야, 이거 [name]에 제품명 넣으니까 대본 바로 나오더라."`
- **Team Usage**: `"오늘 아침 [name] 브리핑 봤어?"`
- **Header + Subtitle Pairing**: `[name] · 올리브영 출신 PM이 만든 K-뷰티 마케팅 AI 파트너`
- **So What? Reaction**: Aim for *"어, 센스 있네 / 기억에 남는다"*, never *"그래서 뭐 하는 툴인데?"* or *"아, 또 그거..."*.
